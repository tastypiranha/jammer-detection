A Hybrid Framework for Jammer Detection Using Statistical Signal Processing
and Polarization Features
================================================================================

Shreyash Puri
Summer Research Fellow
Indian Institute of Technology Madras
July 2026

================================================================================
ABSTRACT
================================================================================

This paper presents a jammer detection framework for wireless communication
systems. The problem is formulated as binary hypothesis testing and addressed
through three progressively challenging scenarios: (i) known jammer statistics
with Bayesian detection, (ii) unknown prior probabilities with minimax
detection, and (iii) unknown jammer distribution with residual-energy
detection. A polarization-based detector exploiting phase-polarization manifold
consistency is developed for strong-jammer scenarios where signal
reconstruction fails. The framework is validated through Monte Carlo
simulations (achieving ~22x residual energy separation), Simulink block-level
modeling, and real-time hardware implementation using HackRF SDR devices at
915 MHz with a Python-based Costas loop receiver. The results demonstrate that
combining statistical signal-domain methods with electromagnetic-domain
polarization features provides a robust detection framework across varying
jammer strengths.

Index Terms -- Jammer detection, hypothesis testing, likelihood ratio test,
residual-energy detection, polarization, software-defined radio, Costas loop.

================================================================================
I. INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

Wireless communication systems are vulnerable to intentional interference,
commonly known as jamming. A jammer emits signals within the operating
frequency band of a legitimate system to degrade communication quality or
disrupt reception. As wireless technologies become critical in civilian,
industrial, and defense applications, reliable jammer detection has become an
important research problem.

Conventional detection techniques based on received power or SNR may perform
poorly when the jammer is weak or when its behavior resembles normal channel
effects. This work formulates jammer detection as a binary hypothesis testing
problem and employs statistical signal processing techniques -- likelihood
ratio tests (LRT), generalized likelihood ratio tests (GLRT), minimax
detection, and matched filtering -- to derive optimal decision rules. The
approach progresses from the most informed scenario (known jammer statistics
and priors) to the least informed (unknown distribution and priors), reflecting
the practical reality that adversaries do not reveal their transmission
characteristics.

Residual-signal analysis is developed by estimating and removing the
legitimate signal from the received waveform. The key insight is that if the
signal can be reconstructed, any remaining energy beyond what noise alone
explains constitutes evidence of jamming. Furthermore, polarization-based
features are utilized, exploiting the vector nature of electromagnetic waves: a
jammer is an independent source with its own polarization state that perturbs
the expected phase-polarization manifold of the legitimate channel.

A. Original Contributions

The major contributions of this work are:

1) Development of Bayesian and minimax jammer detectors under progressively
   relaxed information assumptions, with explicit derivation of optimal
   thresholds and their dependence on system parameters.

2) Design of a jammer-distribution-free residual-energy detector that requires
   no knowledge of jammer statistics, achieving ~22x energy separation between
   hypotheses in simulation.

3) Investigation of a phase-polarization consistency metric for strong jammer
   scenarios where signal reconstruction fails, demonstrating complementary
   performance to residual-based methods.

4) Validation through three levels: Monte Carlo simulation, Simulink
   block-level modeling, and real-time SDR-based hardware experiments at
   915 MHz.

5) Implementation of a complete real-time receiver with Costas loop carrier
   recovery, signal cancellation, and jammer extraction on HackRF SDR
   hardware.

================================================================================
II. DETECTION FRAMEWORK
================================================================================

The jammer detection problem is decomposed into three progressively
challenging cases based on the amount of information available to the receiver.

A. Jammer Detection with Known Jammer Distribution and Known Priors

1) Problem Formulation:

Consider a binary hypothesis testing problem in which a receiver must determine
whether a jammer is present or absent. The received observation is denoted by
y. Two hypotheses are defined as:

   H0: No jammer present
   H1: Jammer present

Initially, we assume that the probability of jammer occurrence is known, the
statistical distribution of the jammer is known, and the costs associated with
wrong decisions are known.

2) Signal Model:

Let the legitimate signal be represented by s(t) and the jammer signal by
j(t). The received signal is:

   H0: y(t) = s(t) + n(t)
   H1: y(t) = s(t) + j(t) + n(t)

where n(t) denotes additive Gaussian noise.

3) Bayesian Detection Framework:

The receiver minimizes the Bayes risk:

   R = sum_{i,k} C_ik * P(decide H_i | H_k) * P(H_k)

The optimal decision rule is the likelihood ratio test:

   Lambda(y) = f_{Y|H1}(y) / f_{Y|H0}(y)  >=< eta

where the threshold is:

   eta = P(H0)*(C10 - C00) / [P(H1)*(C01 - C11)]

4) Example -- Constant Signal and Constant Jammer:

For s(t) = S, j(t) = J, and n ~ N(0, sigma^2), the test simplifies to:

   y >=< gamma

where gamma = S + J/2 + (sigma^2/J)*ln(eta).

The threshold consists of the midpoint S + J/2 between hypothesis means,
adjusted by (sigma^2/J)*ln(eta) which shifts based on priors and costs.

B. Jammer Detection with Known Distribution and Unknown Priors

1) Minimax Principle:

When prior probabilities are unknown, the minimax detector minimizes the
worst-case risk. For equal costs and equal variances, the solution equalizes
false alarm and missed detection probabilities:

   P_FA = P_M

Setting:
   Q((gamma - S)/sigma) = Q((S + J - gamma)/sigma)

yields the minimax threshold:

   gamma = S + J/2

This is the midpoint between hypothesis means, independent of prior
probabilities.

2) Simulation Results:

A Monte Carlo simulation with 10^5 trials was performed. The signal amplitude
A1 = 1, jammer amplitude A2 = 2, and noise variance sigma^2 = 1. The ROC
curve lies above the diagonal, indicating effective discrimination. The minimax
operating point corresponds to gamma = S + J/2, where P_FA = P_M.

C. Jammer-Distribution-Free Residual-Based Detection

1) Motivation:

An intelligent adversary may employ multiple jamming waveforms whose
statistical characteristics are unavailable. Neither the Bayesian nor minimax
detector can be implemented because f_{Y|H1}(y) cannot be specified. This
motivates a detection framework that does not require knowledge of either the
jammer distribution or its prior probability of occurrence.

2) Key Observation:

While the receiver cannot characterize the jammer, it can accurately
characterize the legitimate signal. If the legitimate signal can be estimated
and removed from the observation, whatever remains should contain only noise
under normal conditions. Excess energy in the residual constitutes evidence of
jamming.

3) Residual-Energy Statistic:

Let s_hat denote the signal estimate. The residual is:

   r_k = y_k - s_hat_k

The detection statistic is:

   T_r = (1/N) * sum_{k=1}^{N} r_k^2

Under H0: T_r -> sigma_n^2 (noise variance)
Under H1: E[T_r | H1] = P_j + sigma_n^2 (jammer power + noise)

The decision rule is: T_r > gamma_r => H1; T_r <= gamma_r => H0.

4) Simulation Results:

With N = 100 samples, s_k = 1, sigma = 0.3, and a sinusoidal jammer
j_k = 2*sin(2*pi*k/20), the residual energies were:

   E_H0 = 9.44,  E_H1 = 209.76

The ratio E_H1/E_H0 = 22.2x demonstrates the effectiveness of residual-energy
detection without requiring knowledge of jammer statistics.

5) Limitations:

When jammer power becomes comparable to signal power (P_j >= P_s), signal
estimation degrades. The residual then contains contributions from both jammer
energy and estimation errors, reducing detection reliability.

================================================================================
III. POLARIZATION-BASED JAMMER DETECTION
================================================================================

A. Introduction

To address the limitation of residual-based detection under strong jamming, a
polarization-based detection framework is investigated. The fundamental insight
is that electromagnetic waves possess polarization -- a vector property. A
jammer is an independent electromagnetic source with its own polarization state
that perturbs the expected phase-polarization manifold of the legitimate
channel.

B. Multipath Polarization Model

Assume vertical polarization transmission with a direct path and a reflected
path. Let alpha denote the multipath attenuation, beta the polarization
rotation angle, and Delta_phi the phase difference between paths.

The field components are:

   E_H = alpha * sin(beta) * exp(j*Delta_phi)
   E_V = 1 + alpha * cos(beta) * exp(j*Delta_phi)

The expected polarization ratio is:

   Q_exp = |E_V|^2 / (|E_H|^2 + epsilon)

For alpha = 2 and beta = 5 degrees, the maximum polarization deviation was
found to be Delta_psi_max = 9.96 degrees at Delta_phi = 180 degrees.

C. Jammer Model

The jammer is modeled as an independent polarized source with amplitude A_j,
polarization angle theta_j, and phase phi_j:

   E_{H,j} = A_j * cos(theta_j) * exp(j*phi_j)
   E_{V,j} = A_j * sin(theta_j) * exp(j*phi_j)

D. Phase-Polarization Consistency Metric

The consistency metric is defined as:

   u_k = 10 * |log10(Q_obs(Delta_phi_k) / Q_exp(Delta_phi_k))|

The detection statistic averages over all phase samples:

   u_bar = (1/N) * sum_{k=1}^{N} u_k

Large values of u_bar indicate inconsistency and suggest jammer presence.

E. Detection Rule

   u_bar > gamma => H1 (jammer present)
   u_bar <= gamma => H0 (no jammer)

The threshold is set using a CFAR approach from jammer-free training data.

F. Simulation Results

Monte Carlo simulations (5000 trials) under Rayleigh fading and AWGN show:

- The consistency metric exhibits different statistical behavior under H0 and
  H1, with clear separation between distributions.
- The ROC curve operates above the random-decision diagonal.
- At P_FA = 0.1, P_D = 0.4354.
- Detection probability increases with JSR: P_D = 0.09 at JSR = -20 dB,
  P_D = 0.63 at JSR = 10 dB.

G. Implications for Hybrid Detection

The two approaches exhibit complementary operating regions:
- Residual-based detection performs well for weak jammers (P_j << P_s).
- Polarization-based detection performs well for strong jammers (P_j >> P_s).

This complementarity arises from a fundamental trade-off: signal estimation
quality degrades as jammer power increases, but polarization distortion grows
as jammer power increases.

================================================================================
IV. SIMULINK IMPLEMENTATION
================================================================================

A. System Architecture

The Simulink model implements the residual-based detection framework in a
streaming signal-processing environment:

1) BPSK signal generation
2) AWGN channel modeling
3) Jammer signal injection (sinusoidal)
4) Matched filtering and signal estimation
5) Residual computation via signal subtraction
6) Energy computation (squaring operation)
7) Threshold comparison for detection

B. Key Results

The matched filter provides accurate signal estimation even under combined
noise and jammer interference. Signal cancellation isolates the jammer
component in the residual. The squared residual energy is consistently elevated
above the noise floor when a jammer is present. The oscillatory pattern in the
residual provides a structural detection criterion that complements energy-
based thresholding.

C. Two-Stage Detection Logic

Stage 1 -- Pattern Detection: Examine the residual for oscillatory structure.
   If pattern detected => declare H1 (structured jammer).
   If no pattern => proceed to Stage 2.

Stage 2 -- SNR Monitoring: Compare residual power against expected baseline.
   If power exceeds baseline => declare H1 (noise jammer).
   If power within normal range => declare H0.

================================================================================
V. HARDWARE IMPLEMENTATION
================================================================================

A. Hardware Setup

The experimental setup consists of:
- Transmitter: HackRF One transmitting BPSK at 915 MHz, 2 MHz sample rate
- Jammer: Second HackRF One transmitting sinusoidal or BPSK jammer
- Receiver: HackRF One with Python-based real-time receiver

Parameters: f_c = 915 MHz, F_s = 2 MHz, TX gain = 30 dB, RX RF gain = 20 dB,
RX IF gain = 32 dB.

B. Signal Generation

BPSK signal: 5-second symbol duration, amplitude +/-127 (full 8-bit range),
alternating bit sequence [1, 0, 1, 0, ...].

Sinusoidal jammer: 1 kHz baseband tone, amplitude = 70 (30% power relative to
signal), continuous wave.

C. Receiver Architecture

The Python-based receiver performs:

1) IQ Acquisition: Raw 8-bit samples streamed at 2 MHz via hackrf_transfer.
   Frame size = 500,000 samples (0.25 seconds).

2) Initial Frequency Acquisition: FFT-based coarse frequency estimation with
   parabolic interpolation for sub-bin accuracy.

3) Costas Loop Carrier Recovery: Second-order loop with bandwidth B_n = 1000 Hz
   and damping zeta = 0.707. Implemented sample-by-sample with Numba JIT
   compilation for real-time performance.

   Loop updates:
      omega_{n+1} = omega_n + beta * e(n)
      phi_{n+1} = phi_n + omega_{n+1} + alpha * e(n)

   BPSK phase error:
      e(n) = Q_out(n) if I_out(n) > 0; -Q_out(n) otherwise

4) Signal Reconstruction: Hard decision on demodulated output, re-modulated
   using tracked Costas loop phase.

5) Residual Computation and Jammer Extraction: Signal subtraction followed by
   4th-order Butterworth bandpass filter (500 Hz to 100 kHz).

6) Jammer Power Measurement: P_j = 10*log10(mean(r_I^2 + r_Q^2)) in dBFS.

D. Experimental Results

Before transmission: All outputs show zero (noise baseline established).

After transmission with sinusoidal jammer active:
- Top panel: Stable BPSK demodulation confirms Costas loop lock.
- Middle panel: Clear 1 kHz sinusoidal jammer visible after signal cancellation.
- Bottom panel: Jammer power elevated ~30 dB above noise floor.

The clear transition from zero output to non-zero structured output validates
the residual-energy detector operating in a real RF environment with practical
impairments (frequency offset, phase noise, 8-bit quantization).

================================================================================
VI. CONCLUSION
================================================================================

This work developed a comprehensive jammer detection framework progressing from
Bayesian detection (known statistics) through minimax detection (unknown
priors) to jammer-distribution-free residual-energy detection (unknown jammer
distribution). A polarization-based detector was additionally developed to
address the strong-jammer regime where signal estimation degrades.

The key results are:
(i)   The residual-energy detector achieves ~22x energy separation between
      hypotheses in simulation.
(ii)  The polarization detector provides complementary performance at high JSR.
(iii) The hardware receiver successfully extracts a 1 kHz sinusoidal jammer
      from the residual after Costas loop demodulation and signal cancellation.

Table I: Comparison of Detection Approaches
+---------------------+------------------------+-------------------+------------------+
| Method              | Needs jammer statistics?| Works at high JSR?| Hardware feasible?|
+---------------------+------------------------+-------------------+------------------+
| Bayesian/Minimax    | Yes                    | Medium            | Yes              |
| Residual-energy     | No                     | Weak              | Yes              |
| Polarization        | No                     | Strong            | Harder           |
+---------------------+------------------------+-------------------+------------------+

Future work includes hardware validation of the polarization detector using
dual-polarized antennas, adaptive threshold selection for non-stationary
environments, and evaluation under frequency-hopping and spread-spectrum
jamming scenarios.
